# teeny_reaction_diffusion_notebook
Goofing around with reaction-diffusion models


#Gray–Scott reaction-diffusion patterns you can try to recreate for fun with this model if thats what you think fun is:

## I. Classic Turing Patterns
*Static or quasi-static spatial order emerging from diffusion-driven instability. These are the models of the model.*

**stripes/worms** — V spreads just fast enough to organize into lanes but not fast enough to blobify. F and k in a sweet spot where V is competitive but not dominant. looks like fingerprints, or wood grain, or brains. think about the balance between spreading and dying.
> also: parallel bands, meandering stripes, broken stripes, target-adjacent rings

**labyrinth** — stripes that forgot where they were going. same regime but more disordered, no clear directionality. a small F/k nudge from what is demonstrated here.
> also: maze patterns, tangled worms, aperiodic stripe fields

**spots** — V wins locally and holds its ground in isolated blobs. k is doing more work here — V dies faster at the edges so it can't connect into stripes. looks like leopard print or a petri dish going wrong.
> also: hexagonal dot arrays, pearl patterns, polka chaos

**holes** — inverse of spots. U floods back in fast enough that V can only hold tiny territories. looks like spots but you're looking at the wrong chemical. think about what happens when the food supply is very generous.
> also: bubble arrays, inverse pearls, swiss cheese

---

## II. Excitable / Wave Media
*Patterns generated by propagating activity fronts. Not static. These are alive in a way the Turing patterns aren't.*

**spirals** — rotating pinwheels, rare, lovely and specific. the Gray-Scott equivalent of the Belousov-Zhabotinsky reaction (the red and lilac bromine-y oscillator in a dish). need to be close to an oscillatory regime. think about what happens when a wave front curves back on itself.
> also: target waves — concentric rings expanding outward like a pebble in a pond
> traveling bands, ring waves, scroll waves if you ever go 3D

---

## III. Localized Structures / Dissipative Solitons
*Stable isolated objects embedded in a uniform background. Weird little doo-dadders that shouldn't exist but do.*

**solitons** — stable spots that travel across the grid without changing shape or dying. very specific parameter regime, possibly magic. think: what would it take for V to be stable enough to hold its shape but restless enough to move?
> also: traveling spots, localized pulses, stable isolated islands, gliders (yes like Conway)

---

## IV. Self-Replicating Structures
*Localized structures that divide or bud. The closest Gray-Scott gets to actual biology. a stable spot grows until its edges can't be fed anymore, then pinches off into two. then those two do the same thing. not a mover or stabilizer, a divider.*

what happens at the boundary of a stable spot when the food supply is juuust right... does it hold, or does it reach? Life is wild, innit. Scarcity motivates.
> also: mitotic spots, spot splitting, colony growth, replicating blobs, budding

---

## V. Boundary / Critical Regime Patterns
*Wildcard edgelord cases. Close to instability thresholds, fitful morphology.*

**coral/dendritic** — branchy fractal-ish growth at the edge of the parameter space where the system is almost unstable. V is barely surviving so it grows in thin opportunistic fingers. looks like frost on glass, or neurons, or lightning wanting to happen.

**edge turbulence** — the bulk of the field has its shit together. the boundary does not. where two stable regions meet and refuse to negotiate, the system sits on the precipice between two attractors and just churns and gurns there forever. the interior: calm. the frontier: absolutely losing its mind.
> also: branching fingers, intermittent stripe breakup, diffusion-limited aggregation vibes

---

## VI. Spatiotemporal Chaos
*The system can't decide and never will.*

**chaos** — turbulent churn at parameter boundaries where the system oscillates between two states forever. not a pattern, just an endless argument.
> also: oscillatory disorder, flickering domains, chaotic patch competition, "i should have written down those F/k values"

---

## VII. Absorbing States
*All or nothing terminality.*

**uniform death** — you WILL become acquainted with this. all white. V loses. k too high, or Du too high, or your seed was too small. the soup ran away.

**uniform life** — all one color, V wins everything and there's nothing interesting left. F too low, nothing replenishes U, V eats itself into a monoculture.
